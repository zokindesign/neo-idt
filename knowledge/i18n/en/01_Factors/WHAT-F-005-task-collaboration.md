## Definition
Whether a task requires multiple users/roles to complete it together, and the synchronization, division-of-labor complexity, and information-sharing needs of that collaboration.

## Why It Matters
- Collaborative tasks place special demands on communication, coordination, and role division
- Power imbalances, difficulty synchronizing schedules, and information asymmetry may exist
- Design must foster effective collaboration and reduce its cost

## Quantification Criteria
- **Number of participants**: required headcount range (2+, 2-5, 5+)
- **Synchronization requirement**: must be real-time vs. can be asynchronous
- **Division-of-labor flexibility**: how flexibly the task can be decomposed (fixed roles vs. flexible sharing)
- **Dependency strength**: how tightly the task steps depend on one another

## Design Guidelines
- Clarify roles and permissions to avoid confusion over responsibilities
- Provide shared views with real-time updates (e.g., co-editing)
- Support asynchronous collaboration (comments, task assignment, status tracking)
- Reduce unnecessary communication overhead (automated notifications, progress digests)

## Cross-Domain Manifestations

| Domain | Collaboration Features |
|------|----------|
| **Online documents** | Multi-user simultaneous editing, comments, @mentions, version history |
| **Project management** | Task assignment, progress boards, reminders, document sharing |
| **Social apps** | Group chats, collaborative posts, co-creation |
| **Home spaces** | Shared shopping lists, shared calendars, chore reminders |

## Related Standards
- Cooperative system design principles (e.g., CSCW, groupware)
- ISO 9241-410: support for collaborative work in interactive systems
