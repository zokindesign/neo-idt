## Definition
The economic conditions surrounding the use of a product or service, including users' financial capacity, consumption levels, economic uncertainty, cost tolerance, and the level of economic development in their region — macro- and micro-economic factors alike.

## Why It Matters
The economic environment directly affects whether users can obtain, purchase, and keep using a product or service. Even a highly inclusive design still presents a barrier if users cannot afford it or if the local economy cannot support the required infrastructure. Considering the economic environment helps design solutions that are economically accessible, cost-effective, and adaptable to different economic conditions.

## Related Standards and References
- World Bank income classifications (low, lower-middle, upper-middle, high income)
- National poverty lines and minimum living-cost standards
- Purchasing power parity (PPP)
- The poverty-reduction and inequality targets within the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
- Consumer protection laws and price regulation in various countries

## Suggested Quantification Methods
- User level: disposable income, Engel coefficient, whether below the poverty line
- Regional level: GDP per capita, economic growth rate, unemployment rate, Gini coefficient
- Affordability measures: the maximum users would pay for a specific feature (WTP), tolerance of total cost of ownership (TCO)
- Economic vulnerability indices: e.g., income volatility, savings rate, debt levels
- Data sources: household surveys, World Bank Open Data, national statistics offices, consumer research

## Manifestations Across Domains
- **Digital products**: low-price or free tiers, data-traffic optimization, support for older devices, offline features that reduce network-dependence costs
- **Industrial design**: material substitution to cut costs, modular design for repairability, process choices suited to local manufacturing capability
- **Architectural design**: local materials, simplified construction methods, phased accessibility retrofits
- **Interior design**: furniture rental or sharing models, multifunctional furniture that reduces repeat purchases
- **Graphic design**: double-sided printing, standardized sizes to reduce waste, digital distribution replacing print
- **Fashion design**: durable design that lengthens replacement cycles, easy-care materials that lower maintenance costs, basic styles covering the mass market

## Common Pitfalls and Blind Spots
- Assuming target users have similar economic means
- Considering only purchase price while ignoring the total cost of use, maintenance, and upgrades
- Over-simplifying features in low-income regions, producing "cheap but hard to use" products
- Ignoring the impact of economic volatility on long-term availability (e.g., inflation raising costs)
- Equating "economically accessible" with "low quality", ignoring cost-effectiveness achieved through innovation

## Design Recommendation Keywords
Economic accessibility, cost-effectiveness, total cost of ownership, tiered pricing, lifecycle cost, local production, repair-friendly, sharing economy, essential functions first
