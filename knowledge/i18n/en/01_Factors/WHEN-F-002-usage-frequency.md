## Definition
How frequently users use the product, covering different frequency patterns such as daily, weekly, and occasional.

## Why It Matters
- The needs of high-frequency and low-frequency users differ greatly (feature depth vs. quick onboarding)
- Frequency affects the learning curve, memory retention, and feature-discovery strategies
- Low-frequency users may need more memory aids and revisit incentives

## Quantification Criteria
- **DAU/MAU ratio**: ratio of daily average actives to monthly actives (a stickiness measure)
- **Session interval**: average time between two uses
- **Frequency distribution**: share of high/medium/low-frequency users
- **Duration per session**: average duration of a single use

## Design Guidelines
- High-frequency users: provide shortcuts, batch operations, and entry points to advanced features
- Low-frequency users: simplify the interface, give clear guidance, and show recent-usage history
- Let users set a preferred frequency or auto-adjust difficulty
- Design revisit mechanisms aimed at next-month retention (email, notifications)

## Cross-Domain Manifestations

| Domain | Frequency Adaptation |
|------|----------|
| **Tool software** | Pin frequently used features, history records, saved templates |
| **Social apps** | Adjustable push frequency, aggregated important events, daily digests |
| **Fitness apps** | Periodized training plans, rest reminders, achievement systems |
| **Public services** | Booking systems, saved frequently used information, express lanes |

## Related Standards
- None specific; engagement metrics are common in analytics
