## Basic Definition
A product's or service's environmental impact across its entire life cycle — including energy consumption, carbon emissions, waste generation, and material recyclability — along with the environmental goals the organization sets.

## Why It Matters
- Environmental regulation is tightening, and carbon emissions are becoming a trade barrier
- Consumers and investors increasingly focus on ESG performance
- Green design can lower long-term operating costs (e.g., energy savings)
- It embodies corporate social responsibility and raises brand value

## Quantification Criteria
- **Carbon footprint**: carbon emissions across the production, transport, use, and disposal stages
- **Energy efficiency**: energy consumption per unit of functionality (e.g., per GB of data transfer)
- **Waste rate**: the proportion of recyclable vs. non-recyclable parts
- **Material sourcing**: share of renewable/recycled materials, conflict-minerals screening

## Design Guidelines
- Choose low-carbon materials and processes
- Provide a "green mode" (energy saving, low power, reduced non-essential output)
- Optimize logistics and packaging (reduce volume and weight, use reusable packaging)
- Extend product life (repairable, upgradable, modular)
- Provide end-of-life recycling channels for products

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
| Domain | Sustainable Design Points |
|------|--------------|
| **Digital services** | Green energy for data centers, code efficiency, reduced data transfer volumes |
| **Hardware devices** | Low-power chips, removable batteries, modular design, easily recyclable materials |
| **Apparel and textiles** | Organic cotton, recycled fibers, rental models, secondhand circulation |
| **Architectural spaces** | Passive energy efficiency, solar utilization, rainwater harvesting, green building materials |

## Related Standards
- ISO 14001: environmental management systems
- GHG Protocol: greenhouse gas accounting
- Eco-labels such as the EU Ecolabel and Energy Star
