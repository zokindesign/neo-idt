## Definition
The characteristics of the physical space in which a product or service is used, including natural or man-made physical conditions such as lighting, noise, temperature, spatial layout, accessibility, terrain, and climate.

## Why It Matters
The physical environment directly affects whether users can effectively perceive, operate, and use a product or service. For example, strong light can cause screen glare, noise can interfere with voice interaction, and spatial constraints can block wheelchair access. Considering the physical environment helps design solutions suited to a variety of usage situations, improving reliability and comfort.

## Related Standards and References
- ISO 21542: accessibility of the built environment (corridor width, slopes, lighting, etc.)
- ANSI/ASA S12.60: classroom acoustics standard
- OSHA noise exposure limits
- IEQ (indoor environmental quality) standards: e.g., ASHRAE 62.1 (ventilation), IESNA lighting standards
- Environmental factors in ergonomics: e.g., the effects of temperature and humidity on comfort and performance

## Suggested Quantification Methods
- Lighting: illuminance (lux), uniformity, glare index (UGR), color temperature (kelvin)
- Noise: equivalent continuous sound level (Leq, dBA), Speech Transmission Index (STI)
- Temperature and humidity: dry-bulb temperature, relative humidity, WBGT (wet-bulb globe temperature)
- Spatial accessibility: door width, corridor width, turning radius, slope ratio
- Climate protection: IP rating (dust/water resistance), operating temperature range
- Data sources: environmental measurement instruments, standard test procedures, user context studies

## Manifestations Across Domains
- **Digital products**: anti-glare screens, adjustable brightness, noise-cancelling microphones, dust/water resistance ratings (IP68), readability in bright light
- **Industrial design**: protection ratings, operating temperature ranges, vibration tolerance, easy-to-clean surfaces
- **Architectural design**: slopes, handrails, door widths, stair treads, and non-slip flooring compliant with accessible design
- **Interior design**: furniture tip-over prevention, curtain blackout performance, sound-absorbing materials, damp-proofing treatment
- **Graphic design**: wind- and water-resistant outdoor advertising, detachable structures, weather-resistant inks
- **Fashion design**: waterproof breathable fabrics, thermal insulation, sun protection factor (UPF), quick-drying performance

## Common Pitfalls and Blind Spots
- Considering only ideal laboratory conditions and ignoring the changing conditions of real use
- Assuming all users operate the product in the same physical environment
- Focusing only on extreme conditions (e.g., underwater, high heat) and ignoring everyday mild adverse factors (e.g., a slightly bright room)
- Not considering the combined effects of environmental factors (e.g., high temperature + high humidity)
- Ignoring the impact of vibration and motion on usability in mobile scenarios

## Design Recommendation Keywords
Adaptability, protectiveness, adjustability, durability, environmental compensation, sensory friendliness, anti-glare, noise reduction, easy to clean
