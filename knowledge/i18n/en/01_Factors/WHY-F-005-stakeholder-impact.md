## Basic Definition
The needs, expectations, and power dynamics of the various groups a product or service affects or is affected by (users, customers, partners, regulators, internal teams, communities, and so on).

## Why It Matters
- Design decisions must satisfy not only end users but also customers, channel partners, compliance stakeholders, and others
- Ignoring key stakeholders can lead to market resistance, legal risk, or resource damage
- Balancing competing interests is a common challenge in complex design tasks

## Quantification Criteria
- **Stakeholder map**: positioning on two dimensions — power and level of interest
- **Need-conflict degree**: the level of misalignment between different groups' needs (high/medium/low)
- **Impact score**: the magnitude of the positive or negative impact of a design change on each group
- **Support level**: the strength of each group's support for or opposition to a design proposal

## Design Guidelines
- Identify core users vs. paying customers (which may differ)
- Design customized views or features for different roles (e.g., administrators vs. ordinary members)
- Establish feedback mechanisms that let all parties participate
- Make the basis for decisions transparent, explaining trade-offs

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
| Domain | Examples of Competing Demands |
|------|--------------|
| **Enterprise software** | Management needs reports, employees need convenience, IT needs security |
| **Social platforms** | Users need freedom of expression, regulators need compliance, advertisers need exposure |
| **Healthcare systems** | Patients need privacy, clinicians need efficiency, hospitals need cost control |
| **Educational products** | Students need engagement, teachers need instructional management, schools need procurement compliance |
