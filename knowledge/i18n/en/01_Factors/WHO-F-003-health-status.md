# Health Status
## Definition

Users' physical and mental health states, including chronic conditions, temporary illnesses, allergies, immune-system status, pain levels, fatigue, and sensitivities to particular environments or materials.

## Why It Matters

Health status directly affects users' capability, comfort, and safety. For example, users with respiratory conditions are sensitive to air quality, users with arthritis need low-operating-force designs, and immunocompromised users may avoid touching public surfaces. Considering health status helps design products or services that are safe and comfortable across health states without adding to disease burden.

## Subtypes and Specific Dimensions

### Chronic conditions
- Respiratory (asthma, COPD), cardiovascular, diabetes, neurological (epilepsy, Parkinson's), autoimmune diseases

### Temporary and situational health issues
> B1 cross-contrast reinforcement (INCL-XREF-001, contrasted with Microsoft Inclusive Design)
- **Temporary**: colds/flu, fracture recovery, post-surgical recovery, medication side effects (drowsiness/blurred vision), pregnancy
- **Situational**: altitude sickness, motion sickness, symptoms triggered by bright light/noise, stress-induced symptom flare-ups

### Allergies and sensitivities
- Food/drug allergies, contact dermatitis (nickel/latex/fragrance), multiple chemical sensitivity (MCS), electromagnetic hypersensitivity (EHS)

### Pain and fatigue
- Chronic pain (back pain, arthritis, neuropathic pain), fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue syndrome, cancer-related fatigue

### Urogenital and continence function
> B1 cross-contrast reinforcement (INCL-XREF-001, contrasted with WHO ICF b6)
- urinary/fecal incontinence, urinary frequency/urgency, urinary catheters/ostomy pouches, menstruation- and menopause-related needs
- **Design implications**: bathroom accessibility and trip frequency, ease of removing clothing, tolerance for interrupting long tasks

### Mental health
- Anxiety disorders, depression, post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), obsessive-compulsive disorder, eating disorders
- **Design implications**: tolerance for cognitive load, decision fatigue, tolerance of social pressure, recovery after errors

### Age-related multi-system combined changes
> B1 cross-contrast reinforcement (INCL-XREF-001, contrasted with the Cambridge EDC exclusion calculator)
- **Nonlinear, combined decline** across body systems: cardiorespiratory endurance + muscle strength + sensory acuity + cognitive processing speed + immune resilience
- Comorbidity is the norm: people over 65 carry an average of 2-3 chronic conditions simultaneously
- **Design implication**: modeling a single health condition is insufficient — consider "health combinations" and functional reserve

## Related Standards and References
- WHO International Classification of Diseases (ICD)
- The Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) definition of "major life activities"
- ISO 9999: classification of assistive products (helps interpret health limitations and assistive needs)
- National occupational health and safety standards (e.g., OSHA)
- Health and safety guidance in ergonomics

## Suggested Quantification Methods
- Self-reported health scales: e.g., SF-36, EQ-5D, PROMIS
- Condition-specific measures: e.g., the Asthma Control Test, pain scales (VAS), fatigue severity scales
- Environmental sensitivity testing: reactions to specific chemicals or allergens
- Activity-limitation assessment: how much health conditions impede task completion
- Data sources: health surveys, clinical records (de-identified), user interviews, health-monitoring device data

## Manifestations Across Domains
- **Digital products**: reduced-cognitive-load options, voice control to cut physical operation, rest reminders, avoiding flashing content to prevent seizures
- **Industrial design**: low-operating-force handles, non-slip grips, easy-to-clean surfaces, reduced vibration transmission
- **Architectural design**: accessible restrooms, ample rest areas, air purification systems, low-VOC materials
- **Interior design**: easy-to-clean surfaces, hypoallergenic materials, adjustable furniture for pain or mobility limits
- **Graphic design**: avoiding high-frequency flicker, large clear type, color choices that work for color-vision deficiency
- **Fashion design**: non-itchy fabrics, good breathability, easy dressing and undressing (e.g., hook-and-loop fasteners instead of buttons), no zippers or designs that reduce pressure points

## Common Misconceptions and Blind Spots
- Equating health status with disability alone, ignoring temporary or fluctuating health issues
- Assuming all users are healthy, designing only for the "worst case" and over-engineering
- Ignoring mental-health effects (e.g., how anxiety and depression affect motivation to use)
- Not considering that users may need to seek care at any moment or carry medical devices
- Focusing on a single condition and missing the complexity of comorbidity

## Design Recommendation Keywords
Health safety, low operating force, easy cleaning, environmental friendliness, reduced stimulation, rest reminders, adaptability, accessible hygiene, material safety
