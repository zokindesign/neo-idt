## Definition
The physical barriers users face in reaching, entering, and using a physical space or destination, including distance, path slope, steps, door width, and clearance height.

## Why It Matters
- Geographic barriers can completely block some users from a product or service
- Older adults, people with disabilities, and the temporarily injured are more sensitive to accessibility
- Poor accessibility widens social exclusion

## Quantification Criteria
- **Path width**: clear passage width (wheelchairs require ≥810mm)
- **Slope**: maximum permitted slope (e.g., 1:12)
- **Step count**: whether alternatives (ramps, elevators) are available
- **Distance**: walking distance and time from transit hubs to the destination

## Design Guidelines
- Provide accessible routes (ramps, elevators, handrails)
- Locate key facilities on lower floors or in easily reached positions
- Consider multi-modal access (bus, subway, bicycle)
- Provide remote alternatives (e.g., online services, delivery)

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
| Domain | Considerations |
|------|------|
| **Architectural design** | Entrance ramps, elevators, accessible restrooms, tactile paving |
| **Urban planning** | Accessible sidewalks, reachable public transit stops |
| **Retail spaces** | Mobility scooter aisles, stroller parking, step-free entrances |
| **Public services** | Remote transaction handling, mobile service vehicles, home-visit services |

## Related Standards
- ADA Standards for Accessible Design
- EN 17210: accessibility of buildings
- GB 50763-2012: accessibility design code
