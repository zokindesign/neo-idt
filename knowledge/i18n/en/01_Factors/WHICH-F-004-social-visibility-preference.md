## Basic Definition
Users' preference for how much personal information, activity status, and online presence they expose during social interactions.

## Why It Matters
- Privacy concerns are one of the leading exclusion factors
- Poorly configured social-visibility settings can drive user churn
- Forced social features (e.g., real-name policies) can exclude users who prefer anonymity

## Quantification Criteria
- **Measurement methods**: surveys of privacy-settings usage, selection rates of visibility options
- **Metric**: the proportion of users who change default privacy settings
- **Sensitivity tiers**: different sensitivity levels for personal information, location, activity, friend lists, and so on

## Design Guidelines
- Provide fine-grained visibility controls (independent settings per item of information and per feature)
- Default to the strictest privacy options
- Clearly indicate the current visibility state
- Contextual presets (e.g., "friends only", "work contacts only")

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
- **Social apps**: who can see my posts and online status
- **Wearables**: whether workout achievements are shared to social networks
- **Smart home**: whether household activity logs are shared with visitors
- **Fashion design**: choices over degree of exposure (body coverage, fitted vs. loose)

## Related Standards
- Privacy regulations such as GDPR and CCPA
- ISO/IEC 27701: Privacy information management systems
