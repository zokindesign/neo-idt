## Definition
Whether users can obtain the supporting resources needed while using a product or service, including electricity, water, fuel, cleaning tools, consumable materials, and human assistance.

## Why It Matters
- Resource shortages directly block some functions (e.g., charging, printing, drying)
- Resource constraints are more severe in low-income areas or disaster environments
- Some assistive functions depend on dedicated resources (e.g., a braille printer needs paper and power)

## Quantification Criteria
- **Energy**: stability of the power supply (outage frequency, voltage fluctuation), gas supply
- **Water**: ease of access to clean water, water quality
- **Time**: waiting or preparation time required to obtain resources
- **Cost**: how well the unit cost of resources matches users' ability to pay
- **Substitutability**: whether degraded-function or alternative options exist when resources are unavailable

## Design Guidelines
- Ensure core functions require no scarce resources (e.g., offline capability)
- Label resource requirements clearly (e.g., "requires a printer", "needs power")
- Provide resource alternatives (e.g., disposable batteries instead of charging, handwriting instead of printing)
- Optimize resource consumption (power-saving mode, water-saving mode)

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
| Domain | Example resources |
|------|----------|
| **Digital products** | Electricity, network bandwidth, storage space |
| **Medical devices** | Consumables (needles, filters), power, water for sterilization |
| **Educational products** | Printed textbooks, whiteboards, projectors |
| **Everyday services** | Clean water, paper towels, charging facilities, packaging materials |

## Related Standards
- None specific; context-dependent
