## Definition
The culture, language, religion, values, customs, and social norms of the user's context. These factors shape how the user perceives and accepts a design, what usage habits they form, and their communication preferences.

## Why It Matters
Cultural differences mean that the same design produces radically different experiences across groups. Ignoring cultural background can cause misunderstanding, offense, or barriers to use. Considering cultural factors helps design products or services that are more relevant, respectful of differences, and easier for a broad range of users to adopt.

## Related Standards and References
- ISO 9241-210: human-centred design process (considering the usage context, including culture)
- Hofstede's cultural dimensions theory (power distance, individualism, etc.)
- Common language standards: Unicode, BCP 47 language tags
- WCAG 2.1: language of parts (lang attribute), comprehensibility guidance
- Language requirements in accessibility legislation across jurisdictions (e.g., Canada's multilingual services)

## Suggested Quantification Methods
- Language coverage: number of supported languages, share of target users' native languages covered
- Cultural fit: degree of conformity with cultural norms measured through user research (e.g., color symbolism, icon comprehension, meaning of gestures)
- Localization quality: translation accuracy, correctness of date/time/currency formats, RTL layout support
- Cultural sensitivity audit: scores from expert assessment or focus group feedback
- Data sources: ethnographic studies, cross-cultural user testing, language distribution data from market research

## Manifestations Across Domains
- **Digital products**: interface language switching, date and time formats, cultural fit of icons and symbols, avoiding cultural color taboos, RTL layout support (Arabic, Hebrew)
- **Industrial design**: product appearance aligned with cultural aesthetics, multilingual instruction manuals, consideration of religious taboos (e.g., food-contact materials, patterns)
- **Architectural design**: spatial layouts that fit social etiquette (e.g., living-room placement, prayer space orientation), cultural sensitivity in decorative patterns
- **Interior design**: furniture arrangements matching cultural usage habits, material choices that account for climate and cultural preferences
- **Graphic design**: typographic direction, fonts supporting multiple scripts, imagery that avoids stereotypes, color pairings informed by cultural meaning
- **Fashion design**: styles conforming to cultural dress norms (e.g., degree of coverage, attire for specific occasions), cultural appropriateness of patterns and symbols, material choices accounting for climate and cultural custom

## Common Misconceptions and Blind Spots
- Assuming one "globally universal" design and ignoring local differences
- Translating the language without considering cultural meaning (jokes, metaphors, references)
- Using stereotypes or incorrect cultural symbols
- Ignoring the specific needs of ethnic minorities or migrant groups
- Ignoring cultural taboos in the use of color, shape, or gestures

## Design Recommendation Keywords
Localization, internationalization (i18n), cultural adaptation, symbol review, language switching, RTL layout, cultural consulting, contextual research
