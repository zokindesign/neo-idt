## Definition
The total amount of mental effort a user must invest while carrying out a task.

## Why It Matters
Excessive cognitive load leads to:
- Longer task completion times
- Higher error rates
- More user frustration and abandonment

## Quantification Criteria
- **Measurement methods**: NASA-TLX scale, pupil diameter, task completion time
- **Threshold**: task cognitive load is generally expected to stay at or below a moderate level (NASA-TLX score < 60)
- **Influencing factors**: information density, time pressure, task complexity, technical literacy

## Design Guidelines
- Reduce working memory burden (step-by-step guidance, progressive presentation)
- Provide default values and smart recommendations
- Use clear information layering and visual hierarchy
- Provide undo/redo mechanisms to lower the decision burden

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
- **Digital products**: fewer pop-ups, simplified flows, immediate help
- **Industrial design**: simplified control panels, fewer operating steps, clear labeling
- **Architecture**: clear wayfinding systems, simplified navigation paths
- **Fashion design**: ease of putting on, simplified donning and doffing steps

## Related Standards
- ISO 9241-210: usability engineering standard
- WCAG 3.0: cognitive accessibility guidelines
