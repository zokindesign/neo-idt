## Definition
The financial limits involved in designing, producing, distributing, and maintaining a product or service, including material costs, manufacturing expenses, R&D investment, regulatory compliance costs, and the target selling price or budget ceiling.

## Why It Matters
Cost is a core factor in commercial viability. Ignoring cost constraints can leave a design technically feasible but economically unsustainable, so it never reaches the market or its target users. Balancing inclusive needs against cost control is key to achieving broad adoption.

## Related Standards and References
- ISO 9001: quality management systems (cost-effectiveness considerations)
- Life-cycle cost analysis (LCCA) in government procurement across jurisdictions
- Design for Cost (DFC) methodology
- Disability employment and accessibility tax-credit policies (e.g., the Disabled Access Credit in the US)
- Social return on investment (SROI) analysis frameworks

## Suggested Quantification Methods
- Unit cost: material cost, labor cost, manufacturing overhead
- Development cost: R&D expenses, testing and certification costs
- Compliance cost: the additional investment required to meet a specific standard
- Economies-of-scale effect: the drop in unit cost as output increases
- Willingness-to-pay (WTP) surveys
- Data sources: cost-estimation software, supplier quotes, historical project data, industry benchmarks

## Manifestations Across Domains
- **Digital products**: feature development priorities, third-party service fees, licensing costs, maintenance and update costs
- **Industrial design**: material choice (e.g., plastic vs. metal), manufacturing-process complexity, degree of part standardization
- **Architectural design**: accessibility retrofit costs, elevator installation, cost of dedicated sanitary fixtures
- **Interior design**: prices of aging-friendly furniture, cost of anti-slip flooring materials
- **Graphic design**: multilingual printing costs, cost of special inks or materials
- **Fashion design**: fabric cost for adaptive clothing, cost of bespoke design, feasibility of mass production

## Common Misconceptions and Blind Spots
- Assuming that inclusive design necessarily increases cost, ignoring the scale benefits and spillover returns that universal design brings
- Looking only at upfront investment while ignoring savings in long-term maintenance, support, and avoided retrofit cost
- Failing to account for the affordability and market size of different user groups
- Over-optimizing cost in the early concept stage, making it hard to add necessary accessibility features later

## Design Recommendation Keywords
Cost-benefit analysis, universal design, platformization, modularization, life-cycle cost, economies of scale, prioritization matrix, optional features
