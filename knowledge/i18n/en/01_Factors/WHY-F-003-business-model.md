## Basic Definition
How a product monetizes and captures value — one-time purchase, subscription, freemium, licensing, platform take rate, advertising monetization, and so on — along with the design constraints these create.

## Why It Matters
- The business model directly determines trade-offs in product functionality, the scope of user data collection, and the boundaries of experience design
- Under different models users' willingness to pay and expectations differ, which affects the priority of inclusive needs
- A balance must be struck between profit goals and user value

## Quantification Criteria
- **Revenue-source distribution**: the share of each source (subscription, advertising, transaction take rate, etc.)
- **User conversion funnel**: trial → paid conversion rate
- **ARPU/LTV**: average revenue per user and lifetime value
- **Price sensitivity**: users' response to price changes (elasticity)

## Design Guidelines
- Clearly distinguish free from paid functionality in the design
- Avoid monetization design that overly interferes with the core experience (e.g., ads that obstruct)
- Provide flexible payment options (monthly/yearly, student discounts)
- Consider alternative value-acquisition paths for users with limited ability to pay (time for money, tasks for benefits)

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
| Domain | Manifestation |
|------|------|
| **SaaS software** | Subscription tiers, collaboration seat limits, advanced feature tiers |
| **Hardware products** | Accessory profits, consumable repurchase, software-service bundling |
| **Platform economies** | Take rate, subsidy strategy, member privileges |
| **Public services** | Free basics, value-added services, usage-based billing |
