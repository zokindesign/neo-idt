## Basic Definition
The ways users tend to receive and process information when learning something new or using a new product. Common types include visual, auditory, read/write, and kinesthetic (the VAK/VARK model), plus deeper cognitive styles (field dependence/independence, reflectivity/impulsivity, etc.).

## Why It Matters
- Users with different learning styles vary widely in how quickly and how satisfactorily they understand the same instructional material or onboarding flow
- Offering multiple learning paths reduces training costs and error rates
- Respecting learning-style preferences boosts user confidence and the willingness to keep using the product

## Quantification Criteria
- **VARK questionnaire scores**: score distribution across channels (visual, auditory, read/write, kinesthetic)
- **Learning time**: time to mastery under different modes
- **Test performance**: comprehension test scores after different teaching methods
- **Preference consistency**: whether users' explicit choices among these modes match their implicit behavior

## Design Guidelines
- Offer multimodal content as options (illustrated text, video, explanations, interactive walkthroughs)
- Let guided tutorials switch the learning order on demand (demo → try, or try → hint)
- Let users choose a default help mode (video / illustrated text / interactive guidance)
- Support pace control: repeat, fast-forward, slow down

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
| Domain | Examples |
|------|------|
| **Software use** | illustrated help, video tutorials, interactive walkthroughs, text manuals |
| **Device operation** | pictorial labels, spoken operating cues, hands-on coaching, tactile markers |
| **Spatial wayfinding** | visualized maps, audio guides, written descriptions, in-situ experience |
| **Dressing** | video styling tutorials, illustrated steps, dressing-aid markers |

## Related Standards
- None specific; educational psychology research (e.g., VARK, Kolb's learning styles)
