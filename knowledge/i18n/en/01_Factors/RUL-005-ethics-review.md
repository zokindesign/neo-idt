## Definition
The ethical principles and review mechanisms that a product or service must pass or follow — including user autonomy, informed consent, dignity of data, fairness, non-maleficence, and explainability — especially where user research, data collection, and algorithmic decision-making are involved.

## Why It Matters
- Ethical violations lead to loss of credibility, user backlash, and regulatory intervention
- Bias in AI systems, dark patterns, and manipulative design spark ethical controversy
- Research projects must pass institutional ethics committee (IRB) review

## Quantitative Criteria
- **Ethics checklist completeness**: whether all ethical topics are covered
- **Informed consent rate**: the proportion of users who consent to study terms (and their level of understanding)
- **Algorithmic fairness metrics**: prediction disparities across demographic groups
- **Complaints and disputes**: the number of ethics-related user complaints

## Design Guidelines
- Introduce ethical assessment early (design ethics workshops)
- Provide a clear, understandable informed consent process (not lengthy terms)
- Avoid dark patterns (click inducement, hiding cancellation)
- Ensure algorithmic explainability and a channel of appeal
- Establish an ethics feedback channel

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
| Domain | Ethics Priorities |
|------|----------|
| **User research** | informed consent, privacy protection, participant rights |
| **AI systems** | fairness, transparency, accountability, non-discrimination |
| **Social platforms** | ethics of using user data, fairness of content moderation |
| **Game design** | addiction prevention, gambling mechanics, addictive design |

## Related Standards
- Belmont Report: ethical principles (respect, beneficence, justice)
- GDPR: data protection and consent
- IEEE Ethically Aligned Design
- Institutional IRB guidelines
