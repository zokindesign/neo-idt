## Basic Definition
Users' body dimensions (height, arm length, finger span, etc.) and ergonomic characteristics such as joint range of motion, strength, and endurance.

## Why It Matters
- When equipment or space does not match the user's body dimensions, it easily causes fatigue, operational difficulty, or injury
- Users of extreme height or particular body types may be entirely unable to use a product (e.g., overly tall users hitting door frames)
- Accounting for the diversity of human dimensions improves universal comfort

## Quantification Criteria
- **Percentile reference**: anthropometric data at the 5th, 50th, and 95th percentiles
- **Adjustment range**: the adjustable range and method of seat height, desk height, and screen tilt
- **Operating force**: the pressing force required by buttons, the rotational torque of handles
- **Reach envelope**: whether operating parts fall within the user's reach

## Design Guidelines
- Provide adjustable physical structures (desk and chair, mounts, handles)
- Key operating positions should cover the reach envelope of the majority of the population
- Avoid operations requiring great force or fine control (unless necessary and with an alternative provided)
- Consider special body types such as pregnancy, children, and wheelchair users

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
| Domain | Key Considerations |
|------|----------|
| **Digital products** | Device dimensions and grip feel, touch target size (minimum 44×44pt) |
| **Industrial design** | Tool handle diameter, operating force, control panel layout |
| **Architectural design** | Door frame height, countertop height, stair riser dimensions, handrail position |
| **Fashion design** | Size-range coverage, adjustable parts (waist, leg length) |

## Related Standards
- ISO 7250: basic human body measurements
- ANSI/HFES 100: human factors engineering data
- WCAG 2.5.5: Target Size (touch)
