## Definition
Users' subjective preference for the time periods in which they use a product (e.g., morning, late night, working hours, weekends), which may not match the time actually available to them.

## Why It Matters
- Use during preferred time periods brings higher satisfaction and efficiency
- Period preferences affect UI themes (dark mode at night) and notification timing
- Understanding preferences supports personalized recommendations and user segmentation

## Quantification Criteria
- **Period distribution data**: heatmaps of user active times
- **Self-reported preference**: survey questions such as "When do you prefer to use it?"
- **Period switching frequency**: whether users stick to fixed usage periods
- **Period satisfaction**: experience ratings for different time periods

## Design Guidelines
- Let users set "usual usage periods" to optimize reminders and notifications
- Adjust the interface automatically (dark at night, bright in the morning)
- Offer different content or feature recommendations for different periods
- Respect do-not-disturb settings; avoid interrupting users outside their preferred periods

## Cross-Domain Manifestations

| Domain | Time-of-Day Adaptation |
|------|----------|
| **Content platforms** | Personalized push timing, period-exclusive content |
| **Fitness apps** | Recommended workout times, morning wake-up, sleep-aid meditation |
| **Social apps** | Night mode, good-morning greetings, bedtime reminders |
| **Smart homes** | Lighting and temperature adjusted automatically to user habits |

## Related Standards
- No mandatory standard; behavioral analytics and user research methods
