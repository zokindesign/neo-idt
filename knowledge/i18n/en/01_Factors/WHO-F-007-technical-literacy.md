## Definition
Users' level of knowledge, skill, and experience with technical products or services, including proficiency in operating digital devices, the ability to understand interfaces, the capacity to learn new systems, and adaptability to technological change.

## Why It Matters
Differences in technical literacy exclude some users because operation is too complex or too hard to understand. Accounting for technical literacy helps design products or services that are easy to pick up, cheap to learn, and tolerant of different experience levels, improving accessibility and user satisfaction.

## Related Standards and References
- ISO 9241-210: human-centred design (covering user ability and experience)
- WCAG 2.1: understandability and operability guidance (consistency, error prevention, help documentation)
- Usability principles from human-computer interaction: learnability, efficiency, memorability, errors, satisfaction
- Digital literacy frameworks from institutions such as Peking University
- Digital-inclusion guidance in national aging-response policies

## Suggested Quantification Methods
- Self-report scales: e.g., technology proficiency questionnaires (such as Pew Research Center's technology-use index)
- Performance measures: task completion time, error counts, frequency of help-seeking
- Learning curves: first-use success rate, improvement after repeated use
- Substitution rates: e.g., the share of users choosing a voice assistant over touch
- Data sources: user surveys, usability testing, interviews, behavioral log analysis

## Manifestations Across Domains
- **Digital products**: intuitive onboarding, progressive feature exposure, consistent interaction patterns, clear visual cues, undo and redo
- **Industrial design**: simplified control panels, unambiguous operating indicators, misuse-proof design, ease of maintenance and servicing
- **Architectural design**: clarity of signage systems, usage instructions for elevators and facilities, easily understood emergency evacuation routes
- **Interior design**: affordance cues on furniture and fixtures, simplicity of smart-home interfaces
- **Graphic design**: clear information hierarchy, obvious reading order, universally understandable graphic symbols
- **Fashion design**: easy-to-understand wear instructions, clear indicators on adjustable parts, readable care labels

## Common Misconceptions and Blind Spots
- Assuming all users have above-average technical proficiency
- Treating "intuitive" as the designer's own intuition rather than something tested with target users
- Relying on manuals or training to compensate for complex design
- Ignoring wide differences in technical literacy across age, education, and region
- Focusing only on the first-use experience while ignoring the memory burden of long-term use

## Design Recommendation Keywords
Learnability, consistency, salient feedback, error tolerance, progressive disclosure, contextual help, minimal memory burden, reversible actions
