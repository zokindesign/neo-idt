## Definition
Whether users tend toward a quick overview or an in-depth examination when exploring content or features.

## Why It Matters
- Accommodating different users' exploration modes reduces churn
- Affects interface navigation structure design (flat vs hierarchical)

## Quantification Criteria
- **Measurement methods**: user behavior analytics (page-visit depth, feature usage rates)
- **Metrics**: average time on page, diversity of feature usage
- **Preference segments**: typically divided into "quick finders" and "deep researchers"

## Design Guidelines
- Provide a "quick start" path (default for newcomers)
- Also provide entry points to "advanced settings" or "expert mode"
- Navigation supports multi-level access (breadcrumbs, quick back)
- Give equal weight to search and browsing

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
- **Digital products**: onboarding tutorials vs advanced manuals, prominence of search
- **Knowledge bases**: summary version vs full version, skimming vs detailed study
- **Retail spaces**: curated displays vs detailed exploration areas
- **Clothing selection**: quick outfit recommendations vs detailed inspection of materials and workmanship

## Related Standards
- None specifically; derived from user research segmentation
