## Basic Definition
Users' preferences in visual and sensory presentation style, including color, shape, material, animation density, sound tonality, and more.

## Why It Matters
- Aesthetics are highly subjective; forcing a single style lowers user comfort and sense of identification
- Preferences differ widely across industries and cultural backgrounds
- Offering style choices boosts brand affinity and user stickiness

## Quantification Criteria
- **Measurement methods**: style-preference questionnaires, eye-tracking heatmaps, dwell time, theme-usage statistics
- **Dimensions**: minimal vs. rich, cool vs. warm tones, modern vs. traditional, bold vs. soft
- **Consistency**: whether a user's preference for a single style is consistent across platforms

## Design Guidelines
- Offer multiple official themes (dark/light, high-contrast/soft, dynamic/static)
- Allow customization (colors, fonts, icon sets)
- Keep core feature locations stable across style switches (reducing relearning cost)
- The default style should be broadly acceptable (neutral, highly readable)

## Cross-Domain Manifestations
| Domain | Style dimensions |
|------|----------|
| **Digital products** | flat vs. skeuomorphic, neon glow vs. understated elegance |
| **Industrial design** | streamlined vs. geometric and angular, metal vs. wood |
| **Architectural design** | minimalism vs. ornamental richness, modern vs. traditional |
| **Fashion design** | streetwear, business formal, anime-inspired, retro |

## Related Standards
- No mandatory standards; follow brand guidelines and user research
