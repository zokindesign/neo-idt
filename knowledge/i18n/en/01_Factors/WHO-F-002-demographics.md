## Definition
The basic statistical characteristics of a user population, including age, gender, income level, education, occupation, household structure, geographic distribution, and more. These characteristics shape usage needs, ability distributions, and behavior patterns.

## Why It Matters
Different population groups differ markedly in abilities, preferences, usage contexts, and economic capacity. Ignoring demographic characteristics can produce designs that fail to match the target users' actual needs or that over-focus on a minority group. Considering demographics helps target user groups precisely, forecast the scale of demand, and design products or services with better market fit.

## Related Standards and References
- United Nations demographic classification standards
- National census and social survey methodologies (e.g., the US ACS, Eurostat)
- World Bank data classifications (income level, education level)
- Market research association guidelines (e.g., ESOMAR)
- ISO 20252: Market, opinion and social research

## Suggested Quantification Methods
- Age distribution: mean age, median, share of age groups (e.g., 0-14, 15-64, 65+)
- Gender ratios: male/female ratio, recognition rate of non-binary gender identities
- Education level: shares with less than secondary, with secondary, and with tertiary education or above
- Income level: disposable income per capita, share below the poverty line, Engel coefficient
- Geographic distribution: urban/rural ratio, regional development level indices
- Data sources: official statistical offices, World Bank open data, UN data, market research reports

## Manifestations Across Domains
- **Digital products**: interaction complexity designed per age group (e.g., kids' modes, simplified interfaces for older adults), gender-specific feature needs, regional language and payment-method adaptation
- **Industrial design**: tool sizes scaled to different body proportions; usage contexts that reflect occupational traits (construction worker vs. office worker)
- **Architectural design**: accessible design for aging communities, density and functional zoning of student housing, affordable housing for low-income groups
- **Interior design**: furniture sized to anthropometric percentile data; storage designed around the number of household members
- **Graphic design**: ad targeting, visual styles matched to the target aesthetic group, information density matched to reading habits
- **Fashion design**: size systems covering different body types, styles matched to occupation and lifestyle, price points matched to spending tiers

## Common Misconceptions and Blind Spots
- Treating the user population as a homogeneous whole, ignoring internal differences
- Over-relying on stereotypes (e.g., "all older adults can't handle technology")
- Overlooking emerging groups (e.g., Gen Z, digital nomads) or migrant populations
- Looking only at totals instead of segmented needs (ability variation within a single age band can be enormous)
- Ignoring regional development gaps in global products, producing feature overkill or shortfalls

## Design Recommendation Keywords
Market segmentation, audience personas, life-cycle stages, accessible price points, cultural sensitivity, regional adaptation, demand forecasting, inclusive pricing
