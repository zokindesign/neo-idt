# Shared & Public Use Context

## Definition

The user is not the sole user of the product/space but is situated in a **multi-person shared** or **public setting**. Core dimensions include:

- **Synchronous sharing**: multiple people using it at the same time (home TV, meeting-room display, public self-service kiosk)
- **Asynchronous sharing**: multiple people taking turns (family tablet, shared office desks, library computers)
- **Bystanders / being observed**: the act of use can be seen/heard by others (reading in public, using a phone on the subway, calls in an open-plan office)
- **Account/configuration sharing**: family shared accounts, team shared workspaces, multi-user logins on a device

**Distinguishing it from other factors**:
- WHERE-F-007 (Usage Context) focuses on the physical/social environment of a single user
- WHICH-F-008 (Interaction Mode Preference) focuses on the user's subjective preference for working alone vs collaboratively
- This factor focuses on the constraints that the **objective presence of non-target users** places on privacy, usability, and safety

## Why It Matters

- Family-shared devices: children's accidental purchases, private content seen by family members, personalized recommendations interfering with one another
- Public self-service kiosks: passwords entered under onlookers' eyes, residual traces of operation, time pressure (queues)
- Open-plan offices: screen content exposure, overheard calls, meeting interruptions
- Public transit / outdoors: screen reflections, noise interference, device theft risk

## Design Response (both: alternatives + choice space)

### Constraint components → provide alternatives
- Privacy mode: screen privacy filters, directional audio, automatic clearing of traces
- Guest/temporary mode: no history saved, no pollution of the main account's recommendations
- Lock screen / fast account switching: hide sensitive content with a single action

### Preference/context components → provide choice space
- Personalization isolation on shared devices: multi-user profiles, fingerprint/face fast switching
- Configurable "visibility": work material visible only to colleagues, personal photos only to family
- Public-setting adaptation: automatically enable privacy mode when public Wi-Fi/Bluetooth beacons are detected

## Cross-Domain Mapping

| Domain | Exclusion example | Inclusion example |
|------|----------|----------|
| Digital products | Muddled recommendations on family-shared accounts, screens peeked at in public | Multi-user profiles, guest mode, privacy screen |
| Industrial design | Shared tools with no user identification, public facilities with no privacy design | Multi-user RFID identification, separated public seating |
| Architectural design | Open-plan offices with no privacy zones, large gaps in public toilet stalls | Phone booths/focus pods, all-gender restrooms, acoustic partitions |
| Fashion design | Fitting rooms with no hooks or badly placed mirrors, shared changing rooms | Individual fitting rooms, lockable lockers, privacy curtains |

## Measurement Dimensions

- Completeness of multi-user profiles (whether independent data isolation is supported)
- Availability of guest/privacy mode
- Mitigations for observation risk (privacy filters, directional audio, automatic clearing)
