## Definition
Legal, regulatory, standards, and policy requirements affecting the design, production, distribution, and use of a product or service, including accessibility legislation, data privacy regulations, product safety standards, environmental requirements, and government procurement policies.

## Why It Matters
Policies and regulations are external constraints that design must comply with. Ignoring compliance can leave a product unable to reach the market, expose it to legal action, or exclude it from government procurement. Considering policies and regulations helps mitigate risks early, capture policy benefits (such as tax incentives and subsidies), and ensure the product's legality and sustainability in its target markets.

## Related Standards and References
- Accessibility legislation by country: e.g., the US ADA, the EU accessibility directive, China's Law on the Construction of a Barrier-Free Environment
- Data privacy regulations: e.g., GDPR, CCPA, PIPL
- Product safety standards: e.g., CE marking, UL certification, FCC compliance
- Environmental regulations: e.g., RoHS, REACH, Energy Star
- Government procurement policies: e.g., US Section 508, EU EN 301 549
- Industry self-regulatory standards: e.g., W3C WCAG, ISO accessibility standards

## Suggested Quantification Methods
- Compliance checklist: satisfaction of mandatory requirements derived from applicable laws and regulations (yes/no)
- Certification status: whether required certifications have been obtained (e.g., accessibility certification, safety certification)
- Degree of policy leverage: whether tax relief, subsidies, or government procurement preferences are being captured
- Regulatory update tracking: timeliness of monitoring regulatory changes
- Data sources: legal and regulatory texts, certification bodies, government procurement portals, legal counsel

## Manifestations Across Domains
- **Digital products**: web accessibility legal compliance, data privacy protection (e.g., GDPR), children's online privacy protection (COPPA)
- **Industrial design**: product safety standards, electromagnetic compatibility (EMC), energy efficiency labels, material restrictions (e.g., RoHS)
- **Architecture**: building accessibility codes, fire safety standards, energy efficiency standards
- **Interior design**: furniture safety standards, fire-retardant material requirements, indoor air quality standards
- **Graphic design**: advertising content regulations, mandatory label information, restricted substances (e.g., heavy metals in inks)
- **Fashion design**: textile safety standards, flammability regulations, banned dye restrictions, product labeling regulations

## Common Misconceptions and Blind Spots
- Treating compliance as equivalent to best practice, overlooking that regulations are often minimum standards
- Focusing only on domestic regulations while ignoring the requirements of target export markets
- Treating compliance as a late-stage check rather than an early design consideration
- Overlooking industry self-regulatory standards or customer-specific requirements (e.g., additional clauses in enterprise procurement)
- Lagging regulatory updates leaving designs outdated

## Design Recommendation Keywords
Compliance checking, regulatory foresight, certification readiness, policy leverage, standards tracking, legal counsel, risk assessment, market entry requirements
