## Definition
Users' ability to recognize, understand, use, and create written information, including basic letter recognition, word decoding, sentence comprehension, paragraph reading, and written expression. Literacy level affects how users acquire information and interact through text.

## Why It Matters
Differences in literacy directly determine whether users can understand interface text, instruction text, error messages, and content information. Low-literacy users may rely on graphics, icons, or speech interaction. Considering literacy helps design products or services that present information in varied forms, in clear layers, and without depending on a high level of literacy.

## Related Standards and References
- WCAG 2.1: understandability principle (e.g., readability of language, avoiding overly complex language)
- UNESCO literacy-rate standards
- Adult education frameworks
- Plain Language principles
- Literacy assessment standards from national education departments

## Suggested Quantification Methods
- Literacy assessment: e.g., the National Assessment of Adult Literacy (NAAL), the Programme for the International Assessment of Adult Competencies (PIAAC)
- Text difficulty assessment: e.g., Flesch-Kincaid grade level, Gunning fog index, SMOG index
- Comprehension testing: measuring the proportion of users who correctly understand presented information
- Preference measurement: users' preference for graphic vs. textual information
- Data sources: education surveys, censuses, comprehension performance in user testing

## Manifestations Across Domains
- **Digital products**: icon-assisted comprehension, speech read-aloud options, chunked information, tiered headings, avoiding long paragraphs of text
- **Industrial design**: manual instructions using graphic symbols, warning labels using color coding and graphics, illustrated assembly steps
- **Architectural design**: signage systems using universally understood graphics, room numbers paired with symbols, pictorial emergency evacuation guidance
- **Interior design**: illustrated furniture assembly instructions, easy step-by-step diagrams in instruction manuals
- **Graphic design**: clear information hierarchy, symbols and graphics assisting comprehension, color coding to support information classification
- **Fashion design**: international symbols on size labels, graphic symbols for care instructions (e.g., washing, drying)

## Common Misconceptions and Blind Spots
- Assuming all users have literacy at or above secondary-school level
- Over-relying on text without providing multiple forms of information presentation
- Considering only first-language literacy while ignoring differences in second languages or dialects
- Equating "non-literate" with "unable to understand any information," ignoring alternative graphic and speech channels
- Failing to account for regional literacy differences in global products, making information unreachable

## Design Recommendation Keywords
Plain language, graphic support, speech output, information layering, symbol use, color coding, jargon-free terminology, multimodal presentation
